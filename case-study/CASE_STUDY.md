# Case study: a skill-gap tracker for a Project Consultant role

> **Everything in the "Run" section is hypothetical.** The three people and all their ratings are invented to show how the tracker works. They are not real employees, and the figures are not results from a real team.

## Problem

A team lead needs to know which Project Consultant skills each person still has to build, which skills need training first, and whether training closed the gaps. Without a shared record, those answers depend on memory, and the effect of training can't be shown.

## Approach

1. **Skills from the role.** I took eight skills from a Project Consultant job description, with Project Consultant roles at Godrej Consumer Products in mind: scope and planning, timelines and resources, budget tracking, stakeholder management, risk management, presentation and communication, workshop facilitation and feedback, and the listed tools.
2. **A 1–4 scale with descriptions.** Each skill has a written description of what levels 1 to 4 look like, so two managers rating the same person have the same yardstick. The required level for each skill (2 or 3) is my proposal based on the JD wording.
3. **Manager-confirmed ratings, not self-ratings.** The tracker uses the level the manager confirms.
4. **Simple, checkable rules.** Gap = max(0, required − confirmed). Gap closed = gap before − gap after, only for people rated both times.
5. **Two forms, same rules.** An Excel workbook generated by a Python script (`build/build_tracker.py`), and a single-page web tool (`web/index.html`) for quick use without Excel.

## Run on hypothetical sample data

Three invented people were rated on all eight skills. Persons A and B were then re-rated after (hypothetical) training. Person C was not re-rated, to show how the tracker handles people who haven't been re-assessed yet.

The same data was entered into the Excel workbook and the web tool. Both gave the same figures, shown below.

| Skill | People with a gap | Total gap before | Total gap after | Gap closed | % closed (people re-rated) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Scope & planning | 2 | 3 | 0 | 1 | 100% |
| Timelines & resources | 3 | 4 | 1 | 2 | 67% |
| Budget tracking | 1 | 1 | 0 | 1 | 100% |
| Stakeholder management | 2 | 2 | 0 | 1 | 100% |
| Risk management | 2 | 2 | 0 | 1 | 100% |
| Presentation & communication | 2 | 3 | 2 | 1 | 33% |
| Workshop facilitation & feedback | 3 | 4 | 1 | 2 | 67% |
| Tools (MS Office, G Suite, Monday.com, Zoom) | 1 | 1 | 0 | 0 | – |
| **Total** | **16** | **20** | **4** | **9** | |

What the sample shows:

- **Before training:** 16 open gaps across 3 people and 8 skills (24 ratings), adding up to 20 levels. Each person had 5 or 6 open gaps.
- **Where to train first:** Timelines & resources and Workshop facilitation & feedback have the largest total gaps (4 levels each, all three people).
- **After training (A and B only):** A and B started with 13 levels of gap between them. 9 were closed and 4 remain, so 9 ÷ 13 ≈ 69% of their gap closed.
- **What didn't move much:** Presentation & communication closed 1 of the 3 levels A and B had between them (33%), so it would need a different kind of training.
- **Person C:** 7 levels of gap remain open and aren't counted in the % closed, because C hasn't been re-rated yet.

## What I checked

- The workbook formulas were recalculated in Excel. A single entry of confirmed level 1 against required level 3 gave a gap of 2; a re-rating of 3 gave gap after 0 and gap closed 2 (100%).
- The sample figures above were checked three ways: the Excel workbook, the web tool, and an independent calculation. All three matched.
- In the web tool, Save, reload and Load restored all three people and the same totals.

## What I'd change next

- **Validate the required levels** with someone who hires for the role, instead of relying on one job description.
- **Test it with a real team**, with their consent, to see whether managers rate consistently using the level descriptions.
- **Record dates and the training taken**, so gap closed can be linked to a specific course or project.
- **Export and import between the web tool and the workbook**, so both don't have to be filled in by hand.
- **Show an overall % closed** in the totals row of both tools (today it has to be worked out from the totals).
